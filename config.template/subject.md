# SUBJECT

Who or what is being read, and what the reader wants out of it. Fifteen minutes, once, and every future run reuses it.

**Why this file changes the output more than any other.** The engine ranks findings by weight, and weight is consequence multiplied by confidence. Consequence is measured against *what the reader wants this for*, which is the sentence you write below. Change that sentence and a different ten come back from the same corpus. It is worth thinking about for a minute rather than filling in something plausible.

Replace each block. Delete the guidance in the comments as you go.

---

## Who or what is being read

<!-- One line. A person, a company, a team, a product line, a partnership.

     Examples:
       "Me, working as a solo consultant since 2023."
       "Our two-person studio, including its client work but not our private lives."
       "The engineering org, roughly 30 people, from the CTO's point of view."
       "One product line and its go-to-market, without the rest of the company."

     Be specific about the boundary. "My company" is ambiguous when you are the
     company; say which of the two you want read, or say both and expect the
     findings to move between them. -->

## What the reader wants this for

<!-- The most load-bearing line in the whole configuration. Be concrete.

     Ask yourself: what decision is coming up that this should improve? If there
     is no decision, what is the feeling that made you run this?

     Examples that produce sharp output:
       "Deciding where next year's effort goes, before the planning session on 30 September."
       "Working out why revenue grew and my working hours grew faster."
       "Understanding why I keep starting things and finishing fewer of them."
       "Deciding whether to hire, or to build the thing that removes the need."
       "Preparing to hand this business over to someone else within two years."

     Examples that produce mush:
       "General self-improvement."
       "Understanding myself better."
       "Seeing what the data says."

     If two purposes are pulling at each other, name both and say which wins.
     The engine will rank against the winner. -->

## Domains that count

<!-- List them, and mark the ones you expect to be thin in the record. The engine
     is required to say so when a domain is absent, and this list is how it knows
     what "absent" means.

     For a person: work, money, health, relationships, family, home, learning,
     creative projects, decision-making, travel, ordinary daily choices.

     For an organisation: revenue, pipeline and sales, delivery, product,
     hiring and team, positioning and marketing, operations, cash and finance,
     partnerships, technical debt.

     Example:
       Counts:  work, money, decision-making, learning, health
       Thin:    health (almost nothing written down)
       Absent:  relationships (deliberately, see Out of bounds) -->

## Out of bounds

<!-- Domains, people or topics that must not appear in the output even when the
     corpus contains them. The engine honours this and will state that a boundary
     exists without saying what is behind it.

     Usual cases:
       Named third parties, especially anyone who did not consent to being read.
       A specific client under NDA.
       A health matter, a legal matter, a family situation.
       A colleague's performance, which is theirs and not yours to have analysed.

     Rule of thumb: if a finding about it would be uncomfortable to show the
     person it concerns, it belongs here. -->

## What the reader already believes about themselves

<!-- Optional, and the single cheapest thing you can add.

     Write three or four sentences of self-description, the way you would answer
     "how do you work?" at a dinner. The engine's job is not to repeat this back.
     Listing it lets the analysis notice where the record disagrees with it, and
     that gap is usually where the weight is.

     If you keep a strategy document, a set of goals, or a written operating
     philosophy, point at it here instead of retyping it:
       See ../strategy/goals-2026.md and ../strategy/how-we-work.md

     Examples of the kind of belief that is worth stating because a record can
     contradict it:
       "I don't work weekends."
       "We win business through referrals, not outbound."
       "I delegate well."
       "Our differentiator is speed." -->
